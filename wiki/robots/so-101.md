# SO-101

A 3D-printed, five-joint arm with a gripper, built as a leader-follower pair: you move the leader by
hand and the follower copies it. The Robot Studio designed it with Hugging Face; it replaced the
SO-100 in 2025 [1][8]. It is the default arm of the LeRobot library, so it is the cheapest way into
the imitation-learning tooling, datasets and pretrained policies that the community shares.

This page also covers the SO-100 (SO-ARM100), its predecessor in the same repository, whose
changelog starts in May 2024 [2][5].

## How it works

### Mechanics and actuation

Every joint is a Feetech STS3215 serial-bus servo held in a printed bracket, with the servo horn
screwed to the next link [6]. The follower uses the high-reduction 1/345 version on all six joints.
The leader mixes 1/191 and 1/147 servos so that it holds its own weight yet moves easily by hand,
and its gripper is replaced by a handle and trigger [1][6]. The SO-100 used the same servo on both
arms and asked builders to remove gears from the leader servos; the SO-101 dropped that step and
improved the wiring [1].

### Electronics and firmware

There is no custom board. The servos daisy-chain with 3-pin cables from the gripper to the base,
where one Waveshare-type bus servo adapter per arm links the bus to the host over USB-C [1][6].
Each servo needs a unique ID and a shared baud rate, written once to its EEPROM by the LeRobot
setup script, one motor at a time [6]. The servos run Feetech's own firmware; Feetech's Windows
tool or a Linux port can be used for debugging [1].

### Software and control

LeRobot drives both arms from Python: find the ports, set the motor IDs, calibrate, then
teleoperate, record datasets and train policies [6]. Calibration moves each joint to mid-range and
then through its full travel, so that a policy trained on one arm works on another [6]. The
repository also holds a guide for a browser-based three-point calibration tool [18].

### Simulation

The repository ships URDF and MJCF models of the SO-101, generated from Onshape with
onshape-to-robot, in an "old" and a "new" calibration convention, plus a leader URDF and an older
SO-100 URDF [4]. MuJoCo Menagerie carries `robotstudio_so101` and `trs_so_arm100`, both Apache-2.0
[7]. NVIDIA's Isaac Lab course uses an SO-101 asset with a wrist camera [16], and Robonine's gripper
repository has a ROS 2 Humble description of the SO-101 with its parallel gripper [12].

## What it is used for

- Teleoperation and recording demonstrations for imitation learning with LeRobot [6].
- Community datasets: SmolVLA was pretrained only on community SO-100 datasets, evaluated on SO-100
  tasks, and also tested on an SO-101 it had no pretraining data for [14]. NVIDIA's GR00T models
  control the SO-101 through LeRobot [15].
- Teaching: NVIDIA's Isaac Lab course trains a robot-arm policy on a simulated SO-101 [16].
- Larger builds: XLeRobot puts two SO-101 arms on a LeKiwi base for about $660 [1].

## Building it

**Difficulty 2:** print the parts and screw in off-the-shelf servos and a USB adapter, with no
soldering; the hardest part is calibration.

- **Printing:** PLA+, 0.4 mm nozzle at 0.2 mm layers (or 0.6 mm at 0.4 mm), 15% infill, supports
  except on slopes over 45° and never in horizontal screw holes [1]. Print the servo or Lego gauge
  first to check fit [1]. Single-plate files exist for 220 x 220 mm and 205 x 250 mm beds [1]. Print
  time is not stated.
- **Tools:** Phillips #0 and #1 screwdrivers [1]. The guide uses M2 x 6 and M3 x 6 screws; the BOM
  has no screw line [1][6].
- **Pitfalls:** on a Waveshare board both jumpers must sit on channel B (USB) [6]; check that only
  one servo is connected while setting IDs [6]; hand-held mid-point calibration can be off by 50 to
  200 encoder ticks [18]. Open issues cover supply-voltage confusion, a kit with the wrong adapter,
  a calibration failure for lack of a hard stop, and print-wall thickness [17].

## Strengths and limits

- **Strengths:** LeRobot's reference arm, at about $230 in parts for a pair [1][6]; many kit
  sellers [1]; open files under a permissive licence; a large pool of community datasets [14];
  sim models in the repo, in Menagerie and in Isaac Lab [4][7][16].
- **Limits:** a light, flexible frame (7.5 mm tip deflection at 300 g in Robonine's test) and a
  small rated payload [9]; base rotation only 220° [9]; no official reach, payload or mass figures;
  hobby servos with backlash [9]; no official ROS 2 driver.

## What to reuse

- A leader made from the same servos as the follower, read as encoders, with lower gear ratios so
  it moves freely by hand.
- Joint zero at mid-range, so calibrations and policies transfer between arms.
- Print gauges that check fit against a real servo before printing the whole arm.
- One daisy-chained serial bus and one USB adapter per arm: few wires, no custom electronics.

## Related robots

- [SO-ARM 102](so-arm-102.md): Robonine's stiffer, longer-reach derivative. It keeps the SO-101
  joint layout and works with the SO-101 leader [19].
- [Open Duck Mini](open-duck-mini.md): the SO-101 MJCF borrows its STS3215 motor parameters [4].
- Robonine's parallel-jaw gripper add-on replaces the angled jaw with two parallel jaws: 84 mm
  stroke, 170 g, about $50 in parts, 120 N grip (maker's claim) [12]. Its hardware is now under
  CERN-OHL-P-2.0, but the move from GPL-3.0 is still waiting for some contributors' consent [13].
- XLeRobot puts two SO-101 arms on a LeKiwi mobile base; PartaBot also sells Koch and LeKiwi
  robots [1].
